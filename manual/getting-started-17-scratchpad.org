* Welcome to Lesson 17!

Every other lesson in this guide has taught you to put something in its proper
place: a character in ~objects/characters.org~, a scene in the manuscript, a
plot thread with its own profile and its own crossings.

This lesson is about the opposite. It covers the one file in your project that
asks nothing of you at all, and a convention for the messiest part of writing —
the moment when a question turns out to have three answers and you have no idea
which one is right.

By the end of this lesson, you'll know how to:
- Open and use your project's scratchpad
- Understand why nothing in the package reads it, and why that matters
- Refile the good parts out into your real files
- Branch a question into alternatives and explore each one
- Keep your speculation out of your TODO list

*Prerequisites*: Lesson 1 (you need a project). Everything else is optional —
you can read this lesson at any point after your first project exists.

* Part 1: Finding the Scratchpad

** Opening It

Every project ships one, at the project root:

- English projects: ~scratchpad.org~
- Spanish projects: ~cuaderno-borradores.org~

Open it from anywhere in the project:

#+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
F8 F8 o    →  then pick scratchpad.org from the list
#+END_EXAMPLE

That's the whole interface. There is no capture command, no dedicated
keybinding, no submenu. It is a file, and that is deliberate — the point of the
scratchpad is that it has no machinery attached to it.

** Why You Need One

Look at what happens to a stray thought in every other file:

| If you write it in…            | It becomes…                                     |
|--------------------------------+-------------------------------------------------|
| The manuscript                 | Words in your word count and your daily progress |
| ~objects/characters.org~       | A character the linking system offers you        |
| ~notes.org~ (short story)      | Same — that file *is* the entity file            |
| ~design.org~                   | Part of the Starting Gate you're measured against |

Notice the short story row especially. A short story project's ~notes.org~ looks
free-form, but its Characters and Setting sections are read by the linking layer
and filed into by capture. A half-formed sketch there becomes a half-real
character.

So there was nowhere to put a thought that isn't yet any of those things. Now
there is.

** What to Put in It

Anything, in any shape:

- A "what if" question you can't answer yet
- A fragment of dialogue with no scene to live in
- A scene you fully intend to throw away
- A character who may not exist
- An argument with yourself about whether chapter 9 works
- A list, a doodle, three words and a question mark

There is no template to fill in and no heading you're required to use. The file
ships with a couple of example headings; delete them the moment they're in your
way.

* Part 2: Nothing Reads It

This is the part worth trusting, because it's what makes the file safe.

** Your Word Count Is Unaffected

~F8 F8 a~ walks your *manuscript*. It doesn't touch the scratchpad. Ten thousand
words of abandoned drafting here will not show up in:

- Your ~WORDCOUNT~ properties
- Your progress tracking table
- The writing planner's daily ledger
- The project health report's totals

** Your Exports Are Unaffected

~org-scribe-compile~ (Lesson 11) reads the manuscript and nothing else. There is
no path by which scratchpad content reaches a PDF, an EPUB, or a submission
draft. You cannot accidentally publish your notes to yourself.

** Your Entity Lists Are Unaffected

The linking system finds characters, locations and plot threads by looking in
specific files with specific names. The scratchpad isn't one of them.

So you can write:

#+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
* Maybe there's a sister?

Younger. Estranged. Or maybe she's the one who left, and Alice is the
one who stayed. Try both.
#+END_EXAMPLE

…and "sister" will not appear in your character completion list, will not be
counted as an orphaned entity in the health report, and will not clutter
anything. She becomes real when — and only when — you move her.

* Part 3: Getting the Good Parts Out

** Refiling

When something in the scratchpad turns out to be worth keeping, refile it:

#+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
C-c C-w    →  refile the heading at point
#+END_EXAMPLE

Inside an org-scribe project, ~C-c C-w~ offers *every heading in every file of
the project*, with each candidate labelled by the file it comes from. So a
drafted scene goes straight under a chapter in the manuscript; a character
sketch goes into ~objects/characters.org~; a research question goes into
~notes/research.org~.

Delete the rest without ceremony. That's what the file is for.

** One-Way Only

The scratchpad is a refile *source*, never a destination. You cannot refile
things *into* it, and it doesn't appear in your refile completion list.

This is on purpose. Refiling into a scratchpad would be filing something into a
place with no structure — the opposite of what refiling is for — and it would
add noise to a completion list you use constantly.

* Part 4: Branching a Question

** The Problem

Some questions have one answer and you find it by thinking for a minute. Others
have three plausible answers, and the only way to know which one is right is to
follow each one far enough to see where it goes.

That second kind is hard to hold in your head and easy to lose. You explore
option B, decide it's wrong, and four months later you rediscover option B and
explore it again, because you never wrote down *why* it was wrong.

** The Shape

Give the question a heading and tag it ~:branch:~. Make each answer a child
heading carrying a state. The outcomes of an answer are that answer's children.

#+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
* What if Alice's Need isn't forgiveness?                          :branch:
:PROPERTIES:
:ABOUT:    [[id:char-alice-001][Alice]]
:END:

Her Lie is "if I keep the peace nobody leaves". Forgiveness is the
obvious Need and it's boring.

** CHOSEN A. She needs to stop protecting him
*** OPEN A1. The protection was never asked for
*** OPEN A2. He knows, and has been letting her
** OPEN B. She needs to be believed
   Cheap — collapses into the same beat as chapter 3.
** REJECTED C. She needs to forgive him
   Makes the Ghost redundant. Keeping this here so I don't re-derive
   it in two months.
#+END_EXAMPLE

Nest as deep as the question actually goes. A branch of a branch of a branch is
fine — that's just a question whose answer raised another question.

** The Four States

The scratchpad declares these at the top of the file, so they work there and
nowhere else. Cycle them with ~C-c C-t~ on the heading:

| State       | Meaning                         | When to use it                       |
|-------------+---------------------------------+--------------------------------------|
| ~OPEN~      | Still live, not yet explored    | You've named the option, that's all  |
| ~EXPLORING~ | Being written out right now     | You're following this one today      |
| ~CHOSEN~    | This is the one                 | You've decided                       |
| ~REJECTED~  | Ruled out, with the reason kept | You've decided /against/ — say why   |

~CHOSEN~ and ~REJECTED~ are *done* states, so a settled question folds up out of
your way when you collapse the file, while keeping everything underneath it.

** Keep Your Rejected Branches

The strong temptation is to delete option C once you've ruled it out. Don't.

Why-not is worth as much as why. A rejected branch with its reason attached is
the thing that stops you from spending an afternoon rediscovering it, and it's
often the thing that explains a decision you can no longer remember making. It
costs you three folded lines.

** Your TODO List Stays Clean

Here's the detail that makes this usable day to day.

~F8 F8 5~ (Search TODOs, Lesson 7) scans every ~.org~ file in your project,
scratchpad included. But it only matches the keywords in
~org-scribe-todo-keywords~ — ~TODO~, ~ONGOING~, ~WAITING~, ~TOWRITE~,
~TOREVIEW~, ~REDO~, ~RESTRUCTURE~.

~OPEN~, ~EXPLORING~, ~CHOSEN~ and ~REJECTED~ are deliberately *not* in that list.
So no matter how many open questions pile up in your scratchpad, your TODO
search keeps showing actual work.

Speculation is not work in progress. If you ever add these keywords to
~org-scribe-todo-keywords~, you'll lose that separation.

** Linking a Question to What It's About

The optional ~:ABOUT:~ property holds an ID link to whatever the question
concerns — a character, a plot thread, a scene:

#+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
:PROPERTIES:
:ABOUT:    [[id:char-alice-001][Alice]]
:END:
#+END_EXAMPLE

Because it's a real ID link, it survives renaming the character, and ~C-c C-o~
on it jumps to her profile. Leave it empty while the question isn't about
anything in particular yet.

The easiest way to get the link is to copy it from a scene property you've
already tagged, or run ~M-x org-store-link~ on the character heading and then
~C-c C-l~ here to insert it.

** A Note on Language

In a Spanish project, the file name, the headings and all the prose are Spanish
— but the four state keywords, the ~:branch:~ tag and the ~:ABOUT:~ property
stay English.

That's consistent with the rest of the package: names that software reads are
English in both languages, the same way ~:TARGET_WORDCOUNT:~ is. It also means
the convention reads the same in a bilingual writer's two projects.

* Try It Now

1. Open your project's scratchpad with ~F8 F8 o~.
2. Delete the example branch at the bottom.
3. Write down a question about your story you genuinely haven't answered.
4. Give it three answers as child headings, all ~OPEN~.
5. Pick the one you like least and write one line about why. Mark it
   ~REJECTED~.
6. Pick the one you like most, mark it ~EXPLORING~, and write for ten minutes
   underneath it.

Nothing you write will be counted, exported, or noticed by any other part of the
package. That's the point.
