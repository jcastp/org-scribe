* Welcome to Lesson 11!

You've written your manuscript in org-mode, and now it's time to share it with the world! Whether you're submitting to publishers, sharing with beta readers, or self-publishing, you need to export your work to standard formats.

Org-mode's powerful export system can transform your manuscript into professional documents in multiple formats, and ~org-scribe~ adds special features for creative writing like proper scene break formatting.

By the end of this lesson, you'll have:
- Compiled a clean, readable draft with ~org-scribe-compile~ — the fastest
  path from a working manuscript to something you can actually hand to
  a reader
- Compiled a submission-ready manuscript in standard (Shunn) format,
  with a real contact block, word count, and running header
- Compiled a styled EPUB, with scene breaks that stand out from
  ordinary text
- Understood org-mode's export system for everything compile doesn't
  do yet (full formatting control, EPUB covers and metadata)
- Learned to use the scene break macro for professional formatting
- Exported your manuscript to PDF (via LaTeX)
- Exported to Word-compatible format (ODT)
- Created HTML and EPUB versions for digital reading
- Customized export settings for different requirements

*Estimated time*: 25-30 minutes

* Before You Begin

** What You Need

✓ A manuscript with at least one complete scene
✓ Your writing project open
✓ Understanding of your manuscript's structure (from previous lessons)

** Optional Dependencies

Different export formats need different tools installed:

- **PDF (LaTeX)**: Requires LaTeX distribution
  - Linux/Mac: TeX Live or MacTeX
  - Windows: MiKTeX
  - Check: ~pdflatex --version~

- **ODT (OpenDocument)**: Built into Emacs (no extra tools needed!)

- **HTML**: Built into Emacs (no extra tools needed!)

- **EPUB**: Requires ~ox-epub~ package
  - Install: ~M-x package-install RET ox-epub RET~

Don't worry if you don't have everything - we'll indicate what's needed for each format.

* Compiling a Clean Draft (Start Here)

Before reaching for Org's export dispatcher, try ~org-scribe-compile~
first. It solves the problem most writers actually have at this point:
"I want a readable document from what I've written so far," not
"I want to configure LaTeX."

** Why Not Just Export the Working File?

The manuscript you've been writing in is a *workspace*, not a manuscript.
It has apparatus sections, an eighteen-field property drawer on every
scene, and ~:ignore:~ tags on acts, chapters, *and* scenes. Export that
file directly (~C-c C-e~) without ~org-scribe-compile~ and, unless you've
separately installed ~ox-extra~ (see below), you'll get raw ~TODO Scene 3~
headings in your output — or, even with ~ox-extra~ installed, you'll get
one undifferentiated wall of prose with *no chapter divisions at all*,
because ~:ignore:~ sits on chapter headings exactly as it does on scenes.

~org-scribe-compile~ solves this differently: it *transforms* the
manuscript into a clean intermediate document first — acts dropped,
chapters kept as real visible headings, scenes silenced with breaks
inserted between them automatically — and only then hands that clean
document to Org's own exporters.

** Using It

#+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
F8 F8 X          (or M-x org-scribe-compile)

Compile style:  clean / shunn
Compile the manuscript to:  org / txt / md / odt / pdf / docx / epub
#+END_EXAMPLE

Pick ~clean~ (covered here first) and a format, and org-scribe writes two
files to ~export/~ under your project root (created automatically; worth
adding to ~.gitignore~):

- The rendered output, e.g. ~novel-clean.odt~ or ~novel-clean.pdf~
- The clean intermediate, e.g. ~novel-clean.org~ — always written,
  whichever format you picked. If the output ever looks wrong, open
  this file first: it tells you whether the problem is in the
  transformation or in the renderer.

~org~, ~txt~, ~md~ and ~odt~ need nothing beyond Emacs. ~pdf~ needs a LaTeX
distribution (~pdflatex~ on your PATH), ~docx~ needs the ~pandoc~
command-line tool, and ~epub~ needs the optional ~ox-epub~ Emacs package
(~M-x package-install RET ox-epub RET~ — unlike the others, not bundled
with Org) — all optional, all reported by ~M-x org-scribe-setup-check~.
If one is missing, compile refuses with the tool's name rather than
writing a partial or broken file:

#+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
Cannot compile to docx: `pandoc' is not available
#+END_EXAMPLE

** What You Get

- Acts: omitted (a planning structure, not a reading one)
- Chapters: kept as real headings — "Chapter 1", not "TODO Chapter 1 :ignore:"
- Scenes: silent, with a break inserted between consecutive scenes of the
  same chapter automatically — nothing to type, nowhere to forget it
- Property drawers, dynamic blocks (word-count tables), and ~*EDIT*~/~*NOTE*~
  markers: all gone, not merely hidden from export

** The Scene Break Marker

The default break is ~⁂~ (the asterism, U+2042). Change it with:

#+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
(setq org-scribe-compile-scene-break "· · ·")
#+END_EXAMPLE

or via ~M-x customize-group RET org-scribe RET~, which offers a few named
presets (asterism, three dots, em dash, section sign, fleuron) besides
free text. Two shapes are refused rather than silently corrupting your
manuscript: anything starting with ~*~ (Org reparses it as a heading,
even inside a centered block) and anything starting with ~#~ (an Org
comment, so it would vanish with no trace).

*PDF is the one exception*: it always shows three centered asterisks
(~* * *~) for the break, regardless of this setting. pdflatex's default
fonts don't have a glyph for the asterism (or the fleuron preset) at
all — not an error, just a blank space where the break should be — and
there's no reliable way to check an arbitrary glyph's font coverage
from Emacs. Three asterisks is also the traditional print-manuscript
scene break, so PDF isn't losing anything by not matching your other
formats here.

*EPUB has no such problem* — it's Unicode all the way through, so
whatever marker you've set renders exactly as configured. What EPUB
gets instead is its own styling: a compiled scene break is given a
distinct CSS class rather than looking like an ordinary centered
paragraph, so it reads as a deliberate break in an e-reader. A center
block you wrote yourself (an epigraph, a few lines of verse) is left
with its plain styling.

** Try It Now! (2 minutes)

1. Open your manuscript
2. ~F8 F8 X~, ~clean~, then choose ~txt~
3. Open the resulting file in ~export/~ — notice the chapter headings,
   the scene breaks, and the complete absence of ~TODO~ or property
   drawers
4. Try again with ~odt~ and open it in LibreOffice or Word
5. If you have a LaTeX distribution installed, try ~pdf~ too
6. If you have ~ox-epub~ installed, try ~epub~ and open it in an
   e-reader app or Calibre — check that a scene break looks visually
   distinct from ordinary text

* Compiling a Submission Manuscript (Shunn Format)

"Shunn format" (after William Shunn, who wrote the reference guide) is
what most publishers, agents and magazines mean by "standard manuscript
format": a plain, unfussy contact block, a rounded word count, double
spacing, generous margins, and a running header on every page after the
first. ~org-scribe-compile~'s ~shunn~ style produces this directly from
your manuscript — no separate submission-formatting pass.

** Set Up Your Author Identity Once

Before your first ~shunn~ compile, tell org-scribe who you are. This is
per-author, not per-project — set it once in your init file:

#+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
(setq org-scribe-author-name "Jane Alcott Doe")
(setq org-scribe-author-address '("123 Elm Street" "Springfield, IL 62704"))
(setq org-scribe-author-email "jane@example.com")
(setq org-scribe-author-phone "555-0100")
#+END_EXAMPLE

Only ~org-scribe-author-name~ is required; the rest are optional and
simply omitted from the contact block if unset. Submitting through an
agent? Their contact information replaces your own on the page:

#+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
(setq org-scribe-author-agent '("Pat Agent" "Agent House Literary"
                                "456 Publishing Row" "New York, NY 10001"))
#+END_EXAMPLE

Compiling ~shunn~ with no ~org-scribe-author-name~ set is refused, not
approximated — a submission manuscript with a blank contact block would
be worse than an explicit error telling you what to configure.

** Per-Project Details (Optional)

Two lines in your project's ~.org-scribe-project~ file customize details
Shunn format wants that are specific to *this* manuscript, not to you as
an author. Add them by hand, or with ~M-x add-file-local-variable~-style
editing of the marker file — there's no dedicated command yet:

#+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
# Pen-name: J.A. Doe
# Running-header: TIDE
#+END_EXAMPLE

- ~Pen-name~ — shown as the byline ("by ...") instead of your legal
  name. The contact block still carries your legal name regardless: a
  check gets written to the name on the contact block, not the byline.
- ~Running-header~ — the keyword in "Surname / KEYWORD / page" atop
  every page after the first. Without this marker, org-scribe derives
  one from your title's first word automatically, so you don't have to
  set it just to get a working header — only to change what it says.

** Compiling

#+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
F8 F8 X
Compile style:  shunn
Compile the manuscript to:  org / txt / pdf / docx
#+END_EXAMPLE

~md~, ~odt~ and ~epub~ aren't offered for ~shunn~: Markdown has no page
layout for a submission standard to apply to, ODT support needs a
styles file org-scribe doesn't ship yet, and EPUB is reflowable — there
is no fixed page for a standard manuscript format to describe (the same
reasoning that keeps every export format in this package from
hardcoding a style that might not match what you actually have
installed, or applying a fixed-page standard to a format with no
fixed page).

** What You Get

Every ~shunn~ format shares the same front matter: your contact block
(or your agent's), the word count rounded to the nearest hundred ("about
47,800 words"), and your title centered with "by ..." beneath it. The
manuscript itself closes with a centered "END".

*PDF* additionally gets real page formatting: 1-inch margins, double
spacing, and the running header from page 2 on. This is the closest to
what a publisher expects to see, and needs a LaTeX distribution
installed (~pdflatex~ on your PATH).

*DOCX* (via pandoc) gets the same front-matter content, but *not* the
page formatting — no forced double-spacing or margins. Word documents
get that kind of styling from a reference template, and org-scribe
doesn't ship one to point pandoc at (for the same reason it has never
hardcoded an ODT style name: a wrong one risks corrupting the document
rather than just looking plain). If DOCX with full manuscript formatting
matters for a submission, compile ~pdf~ instead, or export directly to
ODT and apply your own template (see "ODT-Specific Settings" below).

*Plain text* and the *org* intermediate get the front-matter content
with no page model at all — there's no concept of margins or double
spacing in a .txt file. Some submission portals specifically want plain
text, so this is a real target, not just a debugging aid.

** Customizing the Running Header's Appearance

The LaTeX behind the PDF's header and spacing lives in one =defcustom=,
built from stock LaTeX packages only (~geometry~, ~setspace~,
~fancyhdr~ — all in a base ~texlive-latex-recommended~ install, nothing
extra to install):

#+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
(setq org-scribe-compile-shunn-latex-preamble
      "\\usepackage[margin=1.25in]{geometry}
\\usepackage{setspace}
\\doublespacing
\\usepackage{fancyhdr}
\\pagestyle{fancy}
\\fancyhf{}
\\fancyhead[R]{%s / %s / \\thepage}
\\renewcommand{\\headrulewidth}{0pt}")
#+END_EXAMPLE

Keep both ~%s~ placeholders (filled with your surname and the running-
header keyword, in that order) — a customization missing either is
refused with an error naming the problem, rather than silently
compiling a PDF with a broken header.

** Try It Now! (3 minutes)

1. Set ~org-scribe-author-name~ (and, if you like, the address/email)
   in your ~*scratch*~ buffer or ~M-x eval-expression~
2. ~F8 F8 X~, ~shunn~, then choose ~txt~ — read the front matter that
   comes out
3. If you have a LaTeX distribution, try ~pdf~ and look for the running
   header starting on page 2
4. Add a ~# Pen-name:~ line to ~.org-scribe-project~ and compile again —
   confirm the byline changes but the contact block doesn't

* Direct Export with Org's Dispatcher

For PDF with full manual control, EPUB, or fine-grained control that
neither compile style offers yet, export the working file directly with
Org's own dispatcher. This is the "advanced" path: more configuration,
more formats, more manual setup.

* Understanding Org-Mode Export

Before we export, let's understand how it works.

** The Export Dispatcher

Org-mode has a unified export interface:

1. Open your manuscript
2. Press ~C-c C-e~ (org-export-dispatch)
3. You'll see the export menu:

#+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
Org Export Dispatcher
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────

[b] Export to buffer       [l] Export to LaTeX
[h] Export to HTML         [o] Export to ODT
[p] Export to PDF          [t] Export to plain text
[m] Export to Markdown     [e] Export to EPUB (if installed)

[q] Quit
#+END_EXAMPLE

*This is your export command center!*

** Understanding Export Options

Each format has variations:

| Key Sequence | What It Does                               |
|--------------+--------------------------------------------|
| ~C-c C-e l o~  | Export to LaTeX and open file              |
| ~C-c C-e l l~  | Export to LaTeX (don't open)               |
| ~C-c C-e l p~  | Export to PDF (LaTeX → PDF)                |
| ~C-c C-e h h~  | Export to HTML (single file)               |
| ~C-c C-e h o~  | Export to HTML and open in browser         |
| ~C-c C-e o o~  | Export to ODT and open in LibreOffice/Word |

*Pattern*: First letter = format, second letter = action

** Export Scope

You can export:
- **Whole buffer**: Default, exports everything
- **Subtree**: Just current section (with ~C-c C-e C-s~ prefix)
- **Visible only**: Only expanded headings (with ~C-c C-e C-v~ prefix)

*For manuscripts*: Usually export whole buffer.

** What Gets Exported?

- ~:ignore:~ tag on headings → Heading title excluded, content included.
  This is *not* plain Org behavior — it requires the optional ~ox-extra~
  package (part of ~org-contrib~; ~M-x package-install RET org-contrib~).
  org-scribe activates it automatically for your manuscript whenever it
  is installed (=export/org-scribe-export.el=); without it, ~:ignore:~
  does nothing and the raw heading — TODO keyword included — exports
  literally. ~M-x org-scribe-setup-check~ tells you whether it is
  present.
- ~:noexport:~ tag on headings → Heading and content excluded. This
  one *is* plain Org (~org-export-exclude-tags~ defaults to
  ~("noexport")~).
- ~#+OPTIONS: toc:nil~ → No table of contents
- ~#+OPTIONS: todo:nil tags:nil~ → TODO keywords and tags stripped from
  exported headings (shipped in your manuscript template, so a scene
  heading that still says "TODO" in the buffer exports as plain prose)
- ~#+EXCLUDE_TAGS: noexport~ → Tags to exclude

*Your template already has the ~:ignore:~/~:noexport:~ tags and the
~#+OPTIONS~ line configured — install ~org-contrib~ once and exporting
just works.*

* The Scene Break Macro

Scene breaks are crucial in fiction - they show time/location/POV changes within a chapter.

** The Problem

You write in your manuscript:
#+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
Alex walked out. The door closed behind them.

[SCENE BREAK - Later that evening]

Sam sat in the coffee shop, waiting.
#+END_EXAMPLE

But each export format needs different scene breaks:
- **Print (PDF/LaTeX)**: Vertical space (~\vspace~)
- **HTML**: Multiple line breaks
- **ASCII/Markdown/Man**: Three asterisks (***)
- **Word (ODT)**: A centered =***= paragraph
- **EPUB**: Multiple line breaks (it derives from the HTML backend)
- **Any other backend**: falls back to the generic 3-newline default

**Manually changing this for each format is tedious!**

** The Solution: Scene Break Macro

~org-scribe~ provides a macro that automatically converts to the right format.

*** How It Works

1. Your template has this line at the top:
   #+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
   #+MACRO: scene-break SCENE-BREAK
   #+END_EXAMPLE

2. In your manuscript, use:
   #+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
   {{{scene-break}}}
   #+END_EXAMPLE

3. When exporting:
   - LaTeX: Converts to ~\vspace{...}~ (vertical space)
   - HTML: Converts to ~<br><br><br>~ (line breaks)
   - ASCII/Markdown/Man: Converts to ~***~
   - ODT: Converts to a centered =***= paragraph
   - EPUB: Converts to ~<br><br><br>~ (like HTML)
   - Any other backend without a dedicated entry: falls back to the generic default (three newlines)

** Using Scene Breaks

Replace manual scene breaks with the macro:

*** Before (Manual)
#+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
Alex walked out.

***

Sam sat waiting.
#+END_EXAMPLE

*** After (Macro)
#+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
Alex walked out.

{{{scene-break}}}

Sam sat waiting.
#+END_EXAMPLE

** Scene Break Example

Here's a real manuscript snippet:

#+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
,*** Scene 1 - Morning
:PROPERTIES:
:PoV: Alex
:END:

Alex stumbled into the kitchen, eyes half-closed. Coffee. Need coffee.

The machine gurgled to life. While it worked, Alex stared out the window
at the gray morning. Another Monday.

{{{scene-break}}}

Twenty minutes later, coffee in hand, Alex felt almost human. The first
sip was always the best—that moment when the fog started to lift.

{{{scene-break}}}

By the second cup, Alex had a plan for the day. Well, the outline of a
plan. More of a vague direction than an actual plan, but it was a start.
#+END_EXAMPLE

*Three scene breaks* showing:
1. Time jump (20 minutes)
2. Mood shift (foggy → clearer)

When exported, each format gets appropriate breaks automatically!

** Try It Now! (2 minutes)

1. Open your manuscript
2. Find a scene with a time/location jump
3. Add: ~{{{scene-break}}}~ between sections
4. Save the file
5. We'll export it in the next sections!

* Exporting to PDF (via LaTeX)

PDF is the gold standard for print and submission to publishers.

** Requirements

- LaTeX distribution installed (TeX Live, MacTeX, or MiKTeX)
- Verify: ~pdflatex --version~ in terminal

** Step 1: Check LaTeX Configuration

Add LaTeX settings to your manuscript (these are not shipped by default):

#+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
#+LATEX_CLASS: memoir_draft
#+LATEX_HEADER:
#+LATEX_HEADER_EXTRA:
#+END_EXAMPLE

*Note*: Project templates ship with no export configuration at all — that block was removed deliberately (it hardcoded personal absolute paths and an unshipped LaTeX class). ~memoir_draft~ (a draft-oriented variant of ~memoir~, good for book-length manuscripts) and ~article~ (better for short works) are reasonable choices to add yourself; if ~memoir_draft~ isn't available in your LaTeX distribution, use a standard class like ~book~ or ~article~ (see "Custom LaTeX Class" below).

** Step 2: Export to PDF

1. Open your manuscript (~novel.org~ or ~story.org~)
2. Press ~C-c C-e~ (export dispatcher)
3. Press ~l~ (LaTeX)
4. Press ~p~ (PDF)
5. Wait for compilation (10-30 seconds)
6. PDF appears in same directory!

*Full sequence*: ~C-c C-e l p~

** What You Get

The PDF includes:
- Title page (from ~#+TITLE~ and ~#+AUTHOR~)
- Automatically formatted chapters and scenes
- Proper page breaks
- Scene breaks as vertical space
- Professional book-like appearance

** Customizing PDF Output

*** Removing Table of Contents

Add to your manuscript:
#+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
#+OPTIONS: toc:nil
#+END_EXAMPLE

*** Changing Chapter Numbering

Add:
#+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
#+OPTIONS: num:nil
#+END_EXAMPLE

Now chapters show as "Chapter 1" without numbers on sections.

*** Custom LaTeX Class

For specific publisher requirements:

#+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
#+LATEX_CLASS: article        (shorter works - short story template default)
#+LATEX_CLASS: memoir_draft   (book-length manuscripts - novel template default)
#+LATEX_CLASS: book           (standard alternative to memoir_draft)
#+LATEX_CLASS: memoir         (extensive customization)
#+END_EXAMPLE

*** Adding LaTeX Packages

For special formatting:

#+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
#+LATEX_HEADER: \usepackage{geometry}
#+LATEX_HEADER: \geometry{margin=1in}
#+LATEX_HEADER: \usepackage{setspace}
#+LATEX_HEADER: \doublespacing
#+END_EXAMPLE

This creates standard manuscript format (1-inch margins, double-spaced).

** Common PDF Issues

*** "LaTeX command not found"

*Problem*: LaTeX not installed

*Solution*: Install TeX Live (Linux/Mac) or MiKTeX (Windows)

*** "PDF not created"

*Problem*: LaTeX compilation error

*Solution*:
1. Check ~*Org PDF LaTeX Output*~ buffer for errors
2. Look for ~! ~ in the output (error marker)
3. Common issues: special characters, missing packages

*** PDF Looks Wrong

*Problem*: LaTeX class or settings mismatch

*Solution*:
1. Try ~#+LATEX_CLASS: article~ for simpler layout
2. Remove custom LaTeX headers temporarily
3. Export a minimal test document first

** Try It Now! (3 minutes)

1. Open your manuscript
2. ~C-c C-e l p~ (export to PDF)
3. Wait for compilation
4. Open the PDF
5. Check: title page, chapters, scene breaks
6. Note any formatting you want to change

* Exporting to ODT (Word Format)

ODT (OpenDocument Text) opens in LibreOffice, Google Docs, and Microsoft Word.

** Requirements

- None! ODT export is built into Emacs
- To open: LibreOffice (free) or Microsoft Word

** Step 1: Export to ODT

1. Open your manuscript
2. ~C-c C-e~ (export dispatcher)
3. ~o~ (ODT)
4. ~o~ (export and open)
5. File opens in your default ODT editor

*Full sequence*: ~C-c C-e o o~

** What You Get

The ODT file includes:
- Formatted headings (chapters as Heading 1, scenes as Heading 2)
- Scene breaks (vertical space or ***)
- Metadata (title, author)
- Editable paragraphs

*Important*: ODT is fully editable - you can make final tweaks in LibreOffice/Word!

** Customizing ODT Output

*** Using a Custom Style Template

Professional publishers often require specific formatting. You can use an ODT template:

1. Create your formatting in LibreOffice (fonts, margins, headers)
2. Save as template: ~my-manuscript.ott~
3. In your org file, add:
   #+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
   #+ODT_STYLES_FILE: "~/org-scribe/templates/my-manuscript.ott"
   #+END_EXAMPLE
4. Export - your formatting is applied!

*** Manuscript Format Template

Standard manuscript format:
- 12pt Courier or Times New Roman
- Double-spaced
- 1-inch margins
- Header with page numbers

Create this once in LibreOffice, save as .ott, reuse for all manuscripts!

** Common ODT Uses

*** Beta Reader Format

Beta readers often prefer Word format:
1. Export to ODT
2. Add comments instructions
3. Share via email/Google Drive
4. They edit/comment in Word
5. You incorporate feedback in org-mode

*** Publisher Submission

Many publishers accept .docx or .odt:
1. Check publisher guidelines
2. Export to ODT
3. Open in LibreOffice/Word
4. Final formatting tweaks
5. Save as .docx if required
6. Submit!

*** Agent Submission

Literary agents typically want:
- Times New Roman, 12pt
- Double-spaced
- Header with name/title/page number

Use ODT template for this!

** Try It Now! (2 minutes)

1. Open manuscript
2. ~C-c C-e o o~ (export to ODT and open)
3. File opens in LibreOffice/Word
4. Check formatting
5. Try editing a paragraph (it's fully editable!)
6. Close without saving (we'll keep working in org)

1. Open manuscript

** Customizing HTML Output

*** Adding a Stylesheet

For professional appearance:

#+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
#+HTML_HEAD: <link rel="stylesheet" type="text/css" href="style.css"/>
#+END_EXAMPLE

Create ~style.css~ with:
#+BEGIN_SRC css
body {
    font-family: Georgia, serif;
    line-height: 1.6;
    max-width: 40em;
    margin: 2em auto;
    padding: 0 1em;
}

h1, h2 {
    font-family: 'Helvetica Neue', sans-serif;
}

p {
    text-align: justify;
    margin-bottom: 1em;
}
#+END_SRC

Now your HTML looks professional!

*** HTML5 vs XHTML

For modern browsers:
#+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
#+HTML_DOCTYPE: html5
#+END_EXAMPLE

For EPUB compatibility:
#+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
#+HTML_DOCTYPE: xhtml
#+END_EXAMPLE

*** Table of Contents

Enable/disable TOC:
#+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
#+OPTIONS: toc:t      (show TOC)
#+OPTIONS: toc:nil    (hide TOC)
#+OPTIONS: toc:2      (TOC depth = 2 levels)
#+END_EXAMPLE

** Common HTML Uses

1. Export each chapter to HTML

3. Upload to website/blog
4. Share links with readers

*** Digital Preview

Give readers a preview:
1. Export first 3 chapters to HTML
2. Add "buy the book" link at end
3. Share via email/website
4. Beautiful, accessible format

*** Archive Version

Keep a readable web version:
- Export complete manuscript to HTML
- Store in Dropbox/cloud
- Accessible from any device
- No special software needed

* Exporting to EPUB (E-books)

EPUB is the standard format for e-readers (Kindle, Kobo, Apple Books).

** Requirements

- ~ox-epub~ package
- Install: ~M-x package-install RET ox-epub RET~

** Step 1: Check EPUB Configuration

Add EPUB settings to your manuscript (these are not shipped by default):

#+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
#+UID: unique-identifier
#+Subject: Fiction
#+Description: A novel about...
#+Publisher: Your Name
#+License: All Rights Reserved
#+EPUBCOVER: cover.jpg
#+HTML_DOCTYPE: xhtml
#+END_EXAMPLE

** Step 2: Export to EPUB

1. Open manuscript
2. ~C-c C-e~ (export dispatcher)
3. ~e~ (EPUB - if ox-epub installed)

* Customizing Export Settings

Project templates ship with none of this configured — it is deliberately
left for you to add per-project rather than baked into the template (see
[[file:00-feature-index.org][the feature index]] for why). Here's what's
available to add.

** Adding an Export Settings Section

A convenient place to keep these settings together is a dedicated
section, tagged so it never appears in your output:

#+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
,* Export config :ignore:noexport:
#+END_EXAMPLE

Add the ~#+OPTIONS~/~#+LATEX_HEADER~/etc. lines below it, or anywhere
else in the file before the content they affect — Org reads them
regardless of position.

** General Export Options

#+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
#+OPTIONS: ':t *:t -:t ::t <:t H:3 \n:nil ^:t arch:headline author:t
#+OPTIONS: broken-links:nil c:nil creator:nil d:(not "LOGBOOK")
#+OPTIONS: date:t e:t email:nil f:t inline:t num:t p:nil pri:nil
#+OPTIONS: prop:nil stat:t tags:t tasks:t tex:t timestamp:t title:t
#+OPTIONS: toc:2 todo:t |:t
#+END_EXAMPLE

*What these mean*:

| Option    | Meaning                           |
|-----------+-----------------------------------|
| ~H:3~       | Heading depth (3 levels max)      |
| ~num:t~     | Number headings                   |
| ~toc:2~     | Table of contents (2 levels deep) |
| ~author:t~  | Include author name               |
| ~date:t~    | Include date                      |
| ~email:nil~ | Don't include email               |
| ~tags:t~    | Include tags in export            |
| ~todo:t~    | Include TODO keywords             |

** Common Customizations

*** Remove Table of Contents

Change:
#+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
#+OPTIONS: toc:nil
#+END_EXAMPLE

*** Hide TODO Keywords

Change:
#+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
#+OPTIONS: todo:nil
#+END_EXAMPLE

Now scenes marked ~*** TODO Scene 3~ export as ~*** Scene 3~

*** Disable Section Numbers

Change:
#+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
#+OPTIONS: num:nil
#+END_EXAMPLE

*** Include Email

Change:
#+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
#+OPTIONS: email:t
#+END_EXAMPLE

* Preparing for Publisher Submission

Different publishers have different requirements. Here's how to adapt.

** Standard Manuscript Format (Shunn Format)

Most publishers request:
- Times New Roman or Courier, 12pt
- Double-spaced
- 1-inch margins all around
- Header: Last Name / TITLE / Page #
- First page: Contact info top left

*** LaTeX Configuration for Shunn Format

Add to your manuscript:

#+BEGIN_SRC org
,#+LATEX_HEADER: \usepackage{geometry}
,#+LATEX_HEADER: \geometry{margin=1in}
,#+LATEX_HEADER: \usepackage{setspace}
,#+LATEX_HEADER: \doublespacing
,#+LATEX_HEADER: \usepackage{fancyhdr}
,#+LATEX_HEADER: \pagestyle{fancy}
,#+LATEX_HEADER: \fancyhead[L]{Your Name}
,#+LATEX_HEADER: \fancyhead[C]{NOVEL TITLE}
,#+LATEX_HEADER: \fancyhead[R]{\thepage}
#+END_SRC

*** ODT Configuration for Shunn Format

1. Export to ODT
2. Open in LibreOffice
3. Format → Page Style → Headers/Footers
4. Add header with name/title/page number
5. Format → Paragraph → Line spacing → Double
6. Format → Page → Margins → 1 inch all
7. Save as template (.ott)
8. Reuse for all submissions!

** Agent Submission

Agents typically want:
- Cover letter (separate file)
- Synopsis (1-2 pages)
- First 3 chapters or 50 pages
- Standard manuscript format

*Process*:
1. Use subtree export for first 3 chapters:
   - Position cursor on Chapter 1
   - ~C-c C-e C-s~ (subtree scope)
   - ~o o~ (export to ODT)
2. Manually create cover letter
3. Manually create synopsis
4. Combine according to agent's instructions

** Self-Publishing

For Amazon KDP or similar:
- EPUB or MOBI format
- Proper cover image
- Complete metadata
- No manuscript format needed (readers don't care!)

*Process*:
1. Export to EPUB (~C-c C-e e e~)
2. Validate with EPUBCheck
3. Convert to MOBI with Calibre (if needed)
4. Upload to KDP
5. Preview with Kindle Previewer
6. Publish!

** Contest Submission

Writing contests often have strict requirements:
- Anonymous submission (no author name)
- Specific format (usually PDF)
- Page limits
- Specific margins/spacing

*Process*:
1. Temporarily remove ~#+AUTHOR~
2. Set ~#+OPTIONS: author:nil~
3. Add contest-specific LaTeX settings
4. Export to PDF
5. Verify page count
6. Submit!

* Common Export Issues and Solutions

** Problem: Export Creates Multiple Files

*Symptom*: Exporting creates ~novel.tex~, ~novel.aux~, ~novel.log~, etc.

*Cause*: LaTeX creates intermediate files

*Solution*:
- This is normal for PDF export
- Only share the .pdf file
- Delete intermediate files if desired: ~rm novel.{tex,aux,log}~

** Problem: Special Characters Break Export

*Symptom*: Export fails with error about "unknown character"

*Cause*: Some characters need escaping in LaTeX

*Solution*:
- Use org entities: ~\tilde~ for ~, ~\nbsp~ for non-breaking space
- For literal text: ~#+BEGIN_EXPORT latex~ blocks
- Check encoding: save files as UTF-8

** Problem: Scene Breaks Don't Appear

*Symptom*: Scene breaks show as "SCENE-BREAK" literally

*Cause*: Macro not defined or not working

*Solution*:
1. Check top of file has: ~#+MACRO: scene-break SCENE-BREAK~
2. Verify using ~{{{scene-break}}}~ not ~{{{scene break}}}~ (no space!)
3. Confirm ~org-scribe~ is loaded: ~M-x org-scribe-version~
4. The replacement only applies inside an org-scribe context: either the file lives under a project with a ~.org-scribe-project~ marker, or ~org-scribe-mode~ is enabled in the buffer. Outside of that, the literal ~SCENE-BREAK~ text is left as-is (so other org files aren't affected).

** Problem: Export Shows TODO Keywords or Raw Scene Headings

*Symptom*: PDF/ODT shows "TODO Scene 3" instead of "Scene 3", or the
scene/chapter title itself appears in the output where you expected
only its body text.

*Cause*: Either ~#+OPTIONS: todo:nil~ is missing (already shipped in
the manuscript template — check it wasn't deleted), or ~ox-extra~ is
not installed, so the ~:ignore:~ tag that is supposed to drop the
heading title has no effect. Run ~M-x org-scribe-setup-check~ to see
which.

*Solution*:
- Confirm ~#+OPTIONS: todo:nil tags:nil~ is present near the top of
  the manuscript file.
- Install ~org-contrib~ (~M-x package-install RET org-contrib~) for
  ~ox-extra~. org-scribe activates its ~ignore-headlines~ extra for
  your manuscript automatically once it is on the load-path — no
  further configuration needed.

** Problem: Chapters Not Numbered Correctly

*Symptom*: Chapters show as "1.1" instead of "Chapter 1"

*Cause*: Heading numbering style

*Solution*:
- For LaTeX: Use ~\chapter~ instead of ~\section~
- Change heading levels: * = chapter, ** = scene
- Or disable numbering: ~#+OPTIONS: num:nil~

** Problem: Images Don't Appear in Export

*Symptom*: Images show as broken links

*Cause*: Image paths incorrect

*Solution*:
- Use relative paths: ~[[./images/map.png]]~
- Or absolute paths: ~[[file:/full/path/to/image.png]]~
- Verify images exist before exporting

** Problem: EPUB Doesn't Validate

*Symptom*: EPUBCheck reports errors

*Cause*: Invalid XHTML or missing metadata

*Solution*:
1. Set ~#+HTML_DOCTYPE: xhtml~
2. Add all required metadata (UID, Title, Author)
3. Fix reported HTML errors
4. Revalidate until clean

* Summary

You have two ways to get a manuscript out of org-scribe. Start with
~org-scribe-compile~ (~F8 F8 X~): the ~clean~ style needs no configuration
and produces org/txt/md/odt/epub in seconds (epub with its own styled
scene breaks, if ~ox-epub~ is installed), and the ~shunn~ style — once your
author identity is set up — produces a submission-ready manuscript
(org/txt/pdf/docx) with a real contact block, word count, and running
header, generated directly from your working file. Both styles leave an
inspectable intermediate you can check when something looks off. Reach
for Org's direct export (~C-c C-e~) when you need HTML, or configuration
neither compile style offers yet: HTML stylesheets, EPUB metadata and
covers, or hand-tuned LaTeX beyond what ~org-scribe-compile-shunn-latex-preamble~
covers.

The key to successful exporting:
1. Try ~org-scribe-compile~ first — ~clean~ for a quick draft, ~shunn~
   once you're ready to submit
2. For direct export, use the scene break macro for format-independent breaks
3. Understand your target format's requirements
4. Customize export settings in your manuscript (not shipped by default)
5. Test exports early and often
6. Validate output before sharing

Your manuscript is ready for the world - export with confidence!
