* Welcome to Lesson 19!

Everything so far happened inside Emacs. This lesson is about an optional extra
for the moments when you want to /see/ the whole book at once: a page in your
browser with a corkboard, an outline table, a storyline chart, a chapter overview, a story-time timeline, a relationship graph and forms for your
characters, locations and plot threads.

The page does not replace Emacs and does not keep a copy of your work. Every
change you make there is handed to Emacs, which makes it in the buffer you
already know, so undo, saving and git work exactly as before.

By the end of this lesson, you'll know how to:
- Turn the companion on, for Emacs and for one project
- Open it and read the seven tabs
- Move scenes, rename them, and change their state from the page
- Link characters, locations and plot threads to scenes by clicking
- Edit a character's profile in a form
- Understand what happens when Emacs and the page disagree

*Prerequisites*: Lesson 1 (a novel project with scenes) and Lesson 4 (characters).
The demo novel in =examples/demo-novel/= is a good place to try everything.

A short story works too. It has no chapters, so its scenes sit directly under
the =Story Content= heading: the corkboard and the table show them without a
chapter row, the =+= in the act header adds a scene, and there is no Chapters
tab. =examples/demo-short-story/= is the one to try.

* Part 1: Turning It On

Two switches, both off until you decide:

#+BEGIN_SRC emacs-lisp
(setq org-scribe-companion-enable t)
#+END_SRC

in your init file, then, in the project, press:

#+BEGIN_EXAMPLE
F8 B    →  Open the visual companion
#+END_EXAMPLE

The first time in a project, Emacs asks whether this project may use the
companion and remembers the answer as a ~# Companion: yes~ line in
~.org-scribe-project~. If some headings have no ~:ID:~, it offers to add them:
the page can only move or edit what it can address.

Your browser opens a page. Keep Emacs open: it is the one doing the work.

~M-x org-scribe-companion-stop~ ends it. If you restart Emacs or the server,
reopen the page from Emacs (the link carries a one-time token).

* Part 2: The Corkboard

Acts are sections, chapters are rows, scenes are cards. A card shows the title,
the state, the summary, the PoV and plot-point badges, and a word-count bar.

- *Drag* a card to another place in its chapter or into another chapter.
  Keyboard: focus the card and press ~Alt+Up~ / ~Alt+Down~.
- *Click the title or the summary* to edit it. ~Enter~ saves, ~Escape~ cancels.
- The *state dropdown* sets the scene's TODO keyword.
- The ~+~ at the end of a chapter heading adds a scene.
- The ~+~ on an act header adds an empty chapter (heading only, no first
  scene); the title editor opens straight away.
- *Drag a chapter by its handle* (⠿) within its act or into another one;
  keyboard: ~Alt+Up~ / ~Alt+Down~, which crosses to the neighbouring act at
  an edge. Cards fold away while you drag.
- The ~↗~ button (or a double click) shows the scene in Emacs.

Filters at the top (PoV, character, plot thread, location, state) dim the cards
that do not match.

Try it: drag Scene 2 above Scene 1 in Chapter 1, then switch to Emacs and look at
~novel.org~. The whole subtree moved, with its properties and logbook, and the
buffer is marked as modified. Press ~C-/~ once: it is back.

* Part 3: The Table

One row per scene. Choose the columns with the *Columns* button (your choice is
remembered). Click a header to sort inside each chapter (dragging rows is off
while sorted).

- Text fields (summary, timeline, emotion, ...) edit in place.
- Link fields (PoV, characters, plot threads, plot points, locations) open a
  picker listing the entities of that kind, with a search box. Pick, press
  *Done*: Emacs writes proper ~[[id:...][Name]]~ links.
- *Colour by* state or by PoV.
- The *Objects* field works like the other link fields. In Emacs the same
  link is set with ~F8 b~ (Tag Scene) and searched with ~F8 7~.

* Part 4: The Storyline

Scenes run across (numbered, grouped by chapter); characters, plot threads,
locations and plot points run down.

| Mark  | Meaning                          |
|-------+----------------------------------|
| ●     | The character is the PoV         |
| •     | Present in the scene             |
| empty | Absent                           |

Click a cell to add or remove the link. ~Shift+click~ on a character sets (or
clears) the PoV. Click a row name or a scene number to open it in Emacs.

Two warnings come for free: a plot thread absent for more than five scenes in a
row is shaded (the same rule as the plot-thread health report), and a plot point
that no scene serves gets a "No scene uses it" badge.

Entities with a negative ~:Weight:~ are not drawn, but they are never silently
dropped: a line under the group says "Not shown (hidden): ...", and a checkbox
shows them.

** The Chapters Tab

The storyline rolled up to chapters: chapters run across (the act above, the
number below, the title on hover), characters, locations, objects, plot
threads and plot points run down. A cell is the number of that chapter's
scenes where the entity appears; for a character, ~3 (2P)~ means three scenes,
two of them from that character's PoV. Shading shows the share of the
chapter's scenes, and the last column is the total. The tab only reads: click a
cell to open the storyline at that chapter's first scene, a row name to open
the entity in Emacs. With a filter on, only matching scenes are counted and a
chapter with none is dimmed. Empty chapters keep their column, so a number is
always the chapter's place in the book.

** The Relations Tab

Characters are nodes and ~:RelationshipsData:~ entries are typed lines, drawn
from the data each time, so the graph cannot drift from the notebook.

- Click a character, then another, to add a relationship (type suggestions
  come from ~org-scribe-relationship-types~, the project language's list, and
  the types already in use).
- Click a line to change its type or remove the entry.
- Two characters related both ways with the same type share one plain line;
  different types are drawn as two arrows. A target that is no longer a
  character is drawn as a dashed ghost node.
- Layout is not stored, and this tab has no scene filters. A table under the
  graph lists the same data.

Each change is one undo step in Emacs and is not saved until you save.

** Colours

The *Colours* button opens a dialog: pick one of four themes (Default,
Okabe-Ito for colour blindness, High contrast, Muted; each has a light and a
dark variant) and, if you wish, a colour for a role, a relationship type or a
PoV character. A swatch picks the theme's Nth colour; the free colour dialog
pins an exact colour and warns when its contrast is poor. Choices are written
to ~.org-scribe-project~ so they travel with the project; *Reset* removes
them. Warnings and the "scene in Emacs" highlight have their own colours.

* Part 5: Entity Forms

The *Entities* tab lists characters, locations, plot threads and the thirteen
plot points. Pick one:

- *Properties* are the heading's property drawer. Role, thread type and status
  are dropdowns offering the values the capture templates use.
- *Sections* are the text under the entity: its description, and each
  sub-heading (Desire, Need, Plan, Backstory, ...). They are raw org text; the
  heading lines themselves are never changed.
- *Relationships* and *Appears in* list the links and the scenes; click to open.
- Plot points are method prose: you can read them here, and edit them in Emacs.
- The ~+~ next to a group runs the usual capture command in Emacs.

** When Emacs and the Page Disagree

Each section remembers a fingerprint of the text it showed you. If you change
that section in Emacs while the form is open and then save from the page, the
page does /not/ overwrite your Emacs edit: it shows both versions side by side
and asks whether to keep yours or use the one from Emacs.

** The Timeline Tab and Story Time

The *Timeline* tab places scenes by *story time*: what you write in a scene's
~:Timeline:~ property (click the time on a card to change it). Press
~F8 S~ (~org-scribe-set-story-time~) to choose the notation:

- *relative*: ~Day 3~, ~Day 3, 14:00~, ~Night 3, 02:00~, ~Year -9, Day 40~
  (the default; no Day 0, ~Day -1~ is the day before Day 1);
- *dates*: Org timestamps, counted from a Day 1 date it asks for (YYYY-MM-DD);
- *invented calendar*: dates in your own calendar, set up the first time you
  choose it.

You may add a remark after a comma: ~Day 3, 14:00, in the cellar~ is placed
at Day 3, 14:00, and the remark is ignored for sorting and shown as you wrote
it. A remark must start with a letter (so ~Day 3, 25:00~ stays unreadable
instead of being placed on Day 3) and not with ~Day~, ~Night~ or ~Year~. This
applies to the relative notation and to your invented calendar; Org
timestamps do not take a remark.

A scene whose time cannot be read, or that has none, goes to the "no date"
group at the end with the reason. The same data is available as a
~#+BEGIN: story-chronology~ dynamic block in any Org file of the project, and
in the Story Time section of the health report. The page notices a change of
notation at once.

Press ~F8 S~ to see the same data from Emacs: the notation is a project
setting, and the health report (~F8 H~) gains a *Story Time* section
listing scenes with no time, unreadable values and jumps back in time.

* Part 6: Saving, Undo, and Following

- The page never saves your files unless you press *Save* (or set
  ~org-scribe-companion-auto-save~). The header tells you when there are unsaved
  changes in Emacs.
- Each change is one undo step in the buffer.
- As you move point through the manuscript, the matching card or row is
  highlighted (~org-scribe-companion-follow~).
- Changing the state to a keyword that logs a note (such as ~TOREVIEW(j@/!)~)
  records a time stamp instead, so no note window opens while you work in the
  browser.

* Part 7: Options and Safety

An app-style window without browser chrome:

#+BEGIN_SRC emacs-lisp
(setq org-scribe-companion-browser-function #'org-scribe-companion-browse-app-mode)
#+END_SRC

The server listens only on your own machine, behind a random token that changes
on every start, and understands a short fixed list of commands. Nothing is
loaded from the internet.
