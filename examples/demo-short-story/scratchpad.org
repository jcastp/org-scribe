* Loose ideas

The message should be shorter than the reader expects. Nine words at
most. Everything else is Elena deciding what they mean.

Lab at 04:00. Nobody there. The coffee machine is the only thing running
and it has been on all night, which means someone meant to come back.

Don't explain the physics. The story is not about whether it works.

Last three lines: she does the thing anyway. Not defiance — she simply
runs out of reasons not to.

# The verification scene is the most obvious beat available and probably
# the most boring one.  What does the story lose without it?

** REJECTED A. She verifies it, and it checks out

Turns the story into a procedural. The reader stops asking the question
the whole thing runs on.

** EXPLORING B. She never tries

Then the story is about what she was already willing to believe, which
is the secret story underneath. Costs the reader a certainty they may
not forgive losing.

*** OPEN B1. One line acknowledging she could check, and doesn't

*** OPEN B2. It never occurs to her at all

Harder, better, and it makes her someone specific rather than someone
sensible.

** OPEN C. She tries and the result is ambiguous

Wants to be both. Probably is neither.
