* Loose ideas

Sam counts things out loud when nervous. Steps, floors, the seconds on a
crossing light. Alex has never noticed. Try it in the courier handover
scene and see whether it reads as a tic or as a reproach.

Weather: it should rain exactly once, and Alex should be on time for it.

A scene that is just the two care-centre voicemails, back to back, three
weeks apart. No prose around them. Probably too clever. Keep anyway.

"Sometime this week" — Sam repeats it back flat, without the question
mark. That's the whole argument and it's four words.


* What if Alex keeps the 8:00 handover?                            :branch:
:PROPERTIES:
:ABOUT:    [[id:char-alex-rivera-001][Alex Rivera]]
:END:

# The design says Alex asks Sam to take it.  But the Moral Weakness lands
# harder if Alex takes it and is still late — the failure is then Alex's
# alone, with nobody to have asked.

** REJECTED A. Alex takes it and is on time

Kills the Moral Weakness in chapter 2, which is the only thing making
Dana's suspicion reasonable. Also makes the pitch scene unearned: Alex
would already have changed before the Need is stated.

** CHOSEN B. Alex takes it and is forty minutes late

Sam misses the care centre's call anyway, but now there is nobody to
blame, and Alex has to sit with that. Same beat, one fewer excuse.

*** CHOSEN B1. Sam does not mention it

The silence is worse. It also sets up chapter 7, where Sam finally does
mention it and Alex has forgotten which morning is being referred to.

*** REJECTED B2. Sam mentions it that evening

Spends the material too early. By chapter 7 there is nothing left to
detonate.

** REJECTED C. Alex forgets to ask, and Sam covers anyway

Makes Sam a saint and Alex merely careless. The book is not about
carelessness.

Cleanest, and makes Dana an Opponent rather than an obstacle. But it
