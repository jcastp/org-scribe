Chapter 1 finished, and then three hours in design.org, which was the actual

Writing the Stances out worst-to-best solved the cast in one sitting. Five
positions on "who owns your hours", and four of them had nobody holding them.
Dana came out of Stance 2 fully formed — she wants the same eight hours Alex

reason to exist beyond being a nice circle.

made Sam insufferable — a serene person dispensing advice. Left it overnight.

*** What I worked on

The Decision, and the whole Sam problem.

*** What went well

Sam is not a mentor. Sam is running a tighter operation than Alex for reasons
Alex has never asked about, and the notebook is a medication log. The moment
I wrote that down, the second half of the book existed: Bell Lane, the
Thursday call, the assessment, and a moral decision with two goods in it
instead of a lesson.

Which also means the mentor reading is *Alex's mistake*, and the reader
should catch it before Alex does. That is a much better book than the one I
outlined on Friday.

*** Challenges encountered

The watch. I wrote "the watch was still ticking, somehow" and it is a lovely
line and it is wrong: it is mechanical, it has been under the post for two
years, and it cannot be running. Flagged it rather than fixing it — the rule
is that drafting sessions do not become design sessions.

*** What I worked on

Jumped to the end and drafted Full Circle out of order, because I could see
it and the handover scene I was supposed to write is hard.

*** What went well

It works, and it told me something: there is no arithmetic anywhere in it.
Alex has stopped counting. That was not a decision, and now it is one — the
"Running late" thread closes by the counting stopping, and it gets no
announcement at all.

*** Challenges encountered

Writing the end this early means the middle now has to earn it. Also: I
avoided the handover scene, and the handover scene is the one where Alex does
the genuinely unkind thing. Noticing the avoidance is probably the most
useful thing that happened today.

*** Post-Writing Reflection

**** Satisfaction with Today's Work (1-10):

7 for the pages, 4 for the discipline.

**** What to Remember for Tomorrow

Write the handover. Do not soften the ask. Alex has to be likeable while
doing it or the book becomes about a selfish person, which is a smaller book.

*** What I Learned

**** About my story

That it did not have an antagonist for the first four days and I could not
see it. Everything good this week came from one question: *who else wants
these hours?*

**** About my process

I flag rather than fix now. Three *EDIT* markers went into the draft this
week and none of them interrupted a session. Reading them back on Sunday
took eleven minutes and produced two real design changes, both logged in
design.org under Pending design changes.

**** About myself

I avoid the scenes where the protagonist is not sympathetic, and I dress the
avoidance up as inspiration.

* Plot Evolution

How the plot changed from the initial outline:

- The watch stopping moved from the midpoint to the apparent defeat. At the
  midpoint the reader spends the whole third quarter waiting for it.
- Casey was a nameless stranger in the last scene. They now carry Stance 1
  and are in the Coverage table.
- There was no Priya. The Kestrel thread needed someone holding Stance 3, and
  a fake ally is the only way to reveal how the opponent always knew Alex's
  free mornings without breaking the single point of view.
