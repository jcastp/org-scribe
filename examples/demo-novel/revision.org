* Pass 0 — Rest

Let the draft sit for at least two weeks. Distance is the only thing that
turns the Creator into the Critic, and there is no shortcut. Write something
else in the meantime.

* Pass 1 — The dream test

Read the whole manuscript in as few sittings as possible. Mark every point
where you fall out of the dream: you get bored, you get lost, you do not
believe it. *Fix nothing.* Only mark.

* Pass 2 — Theme and structure

- [ ] Is every Stance on the list embodied by someone?
- [ ] Does every Stance receive the consequence the story says it deserves?
- [ ] Does the protagonist begin and end on different Stances?
- [ ] Does the theme emerge through action, or is there preaching?
- [ ] If you framed the theme as a question, what is the statement that
  answers what you actually wrote?
- [ ] Are all thirteen non-negotiables present, each in its own quarter?
- [ ] Are the quarters balanced in length?
- [ ] Are the promises of the first quarter kept?
- [ ] Is progress steady, or are there dead zones?
- [ ] Does the sequence of revelations accelerate, each hitting harder than
  the last?
- [ ] Are there scenes that could be cut or merged?

If it fails → design.org, Core (Theme and Stances) and objects/plot.org.

* Pass 3 — Characters

- [ ] Does every main character have a Ghost, a Lie, a Weakness and a Need?
- [ ] Is the moral Weakness visible in concrete acts against concrete people?
- [ ] Is the Weakness clearly the cause of their failures?
- [ ] Does the opponent want the same thing as the protagonist, and fight
  hard enough?
- [ ] Is their Stance defensible? Could you argue it yourself?
- [ ] Is every change milestone anchored to a specific scene?
- [ ] Does every named character have their Desire in three parts, and is the
  ally's their own rather than auxiliary?
- [ ] Read all of one character's scenes back to back, one character at a
  time. (=F8 F8 2= builds that list.) Does their arc make sense on its own?
- [ ] Are there redundant supporting characters?
- [ ] Are any of them defined so rigidly they can no longer surprise?

If it fails → objects/characters.org.

** Notes

The cast-check Contrast list in objects/characters.org already has one box
unticked ("they show their personality through acts, not descriptions"), with
the offending scenes flagged by *EDIT*: character markers. Start this pass by
running the edit index (=F8 F8 6=) and reading that category first.

* Pass 4 — Scenes and sequels

- [ ] Does every scene have a desire, an opposition and an outcome?
- [ ] What is each scene's Gap: what did the character expect, and what did
  the world give them? If there is no gap, either add one or cut the scene.
- [ ] Does something change in every scene?
- [ ] Does it enter late and leave early?
- [ ] Does it advance plot, arc or theme?
- [ ] Does every scene have a clear emotional core, and connect to the theme?
- [ ] Is there a sequel after every important scene?
- [ ] Does each sequel's decision chain into the next scene's desire?
- [ ] Is anything striking but irrelevant stealing attention?

If it fails → design.org and objects/plot.org.

** Notes

The =:Gap:= and =:Sequel-decision:= properties are filled on every scene in
novel.org, so most of this pass can be read in column view (=C-c C-x C-c=)
before opening a single scene. The COLUMNS line is already set up to show Gap
beside Plot-point.

* Pass 5 — World and symbols

- [ ] Does every setting have at least one Aspect?
- [ ] Are any settings used as scenery only?
- [ ] Does the World Problem show up in every scene, even in the background?
- [ ] Do the symbols change over the course of the story?

If it fails → objects/locations.org and design.org, Symbols.

** Notes

One setting was already cut on these grounds during drafting — a mid-sized
city with a climate and a population figure and no Echo, Hazard or scene. The
=:World-problem:= property on each scene makes the third check mechanical:
any scene with that property empty is a candidate.

* Pass 6 — Stakes and tension

- [ ] Is it clear what is lost if the protagonist fails?
- [ ] Do the stakes escalate quarter by quarter, changing category at every
  step?
- [ ] Could the protagonist walk away from the story without consequences?
- [ ] Is there any chapter with no tension of any kind?
- [ ] Do chapters close on a hook rather than on a summary?
- [ ] Is there variation in pace, or is the tension uniform?

If it fails → design.org, Stakes.

** Notes

The =:Tension-level:= property is filled on every scene, 1 to 10. Reading the
column top to bottom is the fastest version of the last check in this list;
the current draft runs 2, 2, 4, 6, 5, 4, 3, 3, 2, 7, 9, 8, 6, 10, 1.

* Pass 7 — Point of view and dialogue

- [ ] Is the point of view respected? Are there jumps inside a scene?
- [ ] Have you planned any scene this point of view cannot carry?
- [ ] Do the important conversations have a moral layer, and not just a
  melody?
- [ ] Do the characters sound different from one another?
- [ ] Read all the dialogue aloud: is anyone saying exactly what they mean?
- [ ] Do actions contradict words anywhere?
- [ ] Does the reader understand more than any character, where you want
  them to?

If the point of view fails → design.org, Point of view.

** Notes

Single focalizer, so the second check has teeth: Priya's reveal was

- [ ] Mark every explanation that follows a shown moment. (RUE.)
- [ ] And then the detail.

** What to ask

- *Structure*: holes, inconsistencies, confusion? Where did it slow down? Is
  the ending satisfying? Did the transformation feel earned?
- *Characters*: did you care about Alex? Did you believe the choice in the
  lobby? Did you understand Dana? Was Priya's reveal fair?
- *Prose*: info dumps, and where? Slow or overwritten stretches? Unnatural
  dialogue?

** How to read it

Feedback is information, not instructions. Pattern versus noise; symptom
versus cause; and the feedback nobody gives you that you already know is
true.
