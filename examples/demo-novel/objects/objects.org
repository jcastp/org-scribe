* Symbolic Objects

** Marisol's wristwatch
:PROPERTIES:
:Type: Symbol of the story / Symbol of change
:Owner: Alex Rivera (inherited)
:First-appearance: Chapter 2, Scene 1
:Status: Stopped for the first two-thirds of the book
:END:

*** Physical Description

A 1960s mechanical wristwatch on a worn leather strap, small by modern
standards, with a face that has yellowed evenly. No battery, no alarm, no
date. It runs for about thirty-six hours on a full wind and gives no warning
before it stops.

*** Origin

Marisol Rivera wore it every day for fifty years. It has been under the mail
pile on Alex's coffee table for two of them.

*** Importance in the Plot

It is the routine, not a metaphor for it. A mechanical watch has to be wound
by hand, at roughly the same time, every day, or it silently stops being
right. Everything the book argues about days is already true of this object.

*** Object History

- Chapter 2, Scene 1 :: Found under the mail. Alex puts it on and does not
  wind it.
- Chapters 3–5 :: Worn daily, never wound. It is right twice a day and Alex
  never notices, because Alex does not check.
- Chapter 6, Scene 2 :: Alex looks at it properly for the first time. It
  reads 8:00 at twenty to eleven at night.
- Chapter 7, Scene 1 :: Wound. Forty seconds, badly, and it is the whole
  self-revelation.
- Chapter 8, Scene 2 :: Wound every morning, unremarked.

*** Symbolism

Of the story, and of change. The two are the same object on purpose: the
thing that stands for the theme is also the thing that visibly alters.

*** Rules and limitations

Thirty-six hours. It does not announce that it has stopped. See
objects/worldbuilding.org, "Rules and limits" — a rule with no cost is not a
rule, and this one costs Alex the moment they finally read the face.

*** Notes

Two continuity errors are currently in the draft: Chapter 2 Scene 1 says the
watch is "still ticking", and Chapter 3 Scene 1 gives it an alarm. Both are
flagged with *EDIT*: plot markers in novel.org and will show up in the edit
index (=F8 F8 6=).

** Sam's notebook
:PROPERTIES:
:Type: Symbol of character
:Owner: Sam Chen
:First-appearance: Chapter 1, Scene 2
:Status: Never opened on the page until Chapter 6
:END:

*** Physical Description

Small, paper, softcover, and full. Rubber band around it. Sam takes it out
in company and does not open it.

*** Importance in the Plot

It is the book's one deliberate misreading. For five chapters the reader
sees, through Alex, an emblem of a well-organized life. In Chapter 6 it is
opened and it is a medication log going back fourteen months.

*** Symbolism

Of character — for Sam, and by mistake for Alex, who has been aspiring to an
object whose contents they never asked about. The mentor reading is Alex's
error and the notebook is where it is stored.

*** Rules and limitations

It must be visible early, often, and never opened. If a single line in Act I
implies its contents, the Chapter 6 reveal becomes a confirmation instead of
a correction.

*** Notes

Paired with the watch on purpose: one object that must be maintained daily,
one object that records that maintenance. Alex acquires both, in that order.

* Relevant Everyday Objects

** The unmade bed
:PROPERTIES:
:Type: Symbol of action
:Owner: Alex Rivera
:First-appearance: Chapter 1, Scene 1
:Status: Made from Chapter 3 onward, meant from Chapter 7 onward
:END:

The same act three times, meaning something different each time: absurd in
Act I, performed for an audience of one in Act II, sincere in Act III. It is
the cheapest symbol in the book and does the most work per word.

:Type: Symbol of world

A number that only ever goes up. It is the World Problem rendered as an
