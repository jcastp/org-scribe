* Detailed Events

** The Thursday call
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: tl-thursday-call-001
:Type: Recurring appointment
:Relevance: Critical
:Date: Every Thursday
:Time: 08:00
:Duration: Under five minutes
:Characters: Sam Chen
:Location: Bell Lane Day Centre (by telephone)
:Chapter: Established Ch. 2, missed Ch. 6
:END:

*** Description

Bell Lane calls at eight on Thursdays to confirm the following week's
provisional place. It rings twice, a minute apart, and then it does not ring
again until the next Thursday.

*** Consequences

Two consecutive misses drop the name to the bottom of the list. Sam misses
one in Chapter 6 and had already missed one, unremarked, in the week of
Chapter 2 — which is why one miss is fatal here and the reader can work that
out on a reread.

*** Connections

This is the immovable hour the whole book is arranged around. Alex spends it
three times: Chapter 2 Scene 2 (refused), Chapter 6 Scene 1 (granted, and
fatal), and Chapter 7 Scene 1 (given back, with a number attached).

*** Notes

The five-minute duration matters. It is a *small* hour, and that is the
point: what Alex has been taking is never large.

** The assessment appointment
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: tl-assessment-001
:Type: One-off appointment
:Relevance: Critical
:Date: Day 25
:Time: 09:00
:Duration: 90 minutes, in person
:Characters: Sam Chen, Sam's father
:Location: Bell Lane Day Centre
:Chapter: Ch. 7 (offered), Ch. 8 (kept)
:END:

*** Description

Rescheduled once after the missed call, to a nine o'clock that cannot move
again. Twenty-five minutes' drive from Okonkwo & Field.

*** Consequences

It collides exactly with the Kestrel pitch, which is the moral decision. The
collision must be *arranged by the world*, not by Dana — if Dana schedules
the pitch to trap Alex, the decision becomes a response to a villain instead
of a choice between two goods.

*** Connections

See design.org, "The ending": Alex keeps this and loses the retainer.

*** Notes

The appointment itself is never dramatized. The reader sees Alex drive and
does not see Bell Lane, because the scene is about the leaving.

** The morning Marisol died
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: tl-marisol-001
:Type: The Ghost
:Relevance: Critical
:Date: Two years before Day 1
:Time: 09:00 promised, 10:40 died, 11:00 arrived
:Duration: One morning
:Characters: Alex Rivera, Marisol Rivera
:Location: Hospice (never named, never shown)
:Chapter: Delivered in pieces across the book; in order only in Ch. 7
:END:

*** Description

Alex promised nine. Three alarms, a shower that ran long, a client message
answered on the stairs. Eleven o'clock. She died at ten-forty.

*** Consequences

The Lie: that clocks are cruel and that time cannot be held. Every behaviour
in Act I follows from it.

*** Connections

Never written as a flashback scene. It arrives as fragments — a bare wrist in
Chapter 1, a half-sentence in Chapter 4 — and is assembled in the correct
order exactly once, in Chapter 7, which is what makes that assembly the
self-revelation.

*** Notes

The reader should have all the pieces before Chapter 7 and should not have
put them in order either.

See the Backstory section of Alex's profile in objects/characters.org. Alex
