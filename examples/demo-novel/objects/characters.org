* Alex Rivera
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: char-alex-rivera-001
:Role: Protagonist
:Stance: Promise nobody an hour
:Goal: Win the Kestrel retainer
:Age: 28
:Occupation: Freelance graphic designer
:First-appearance: Chapter 1, Scene 1
:Weight: 1
:RelationshipsData: [[id:char-sam-chen-001][Sam Chen]](neighbour,7,positive); [[id:char-dana-okonkwo-001][Dana Okonkwo]](client,8,negative); [[id:char-priya-raman-001][Priya Raman]](colleague,4,positive); [[id:char-marisol-rivera-001][Marisol Rivera]](family,10,positive)
:END:

- Concept :: A designer who has never once been where they said they would
  be, and has never once meant to let anyone down.
- Stance :: "The hours aren't mine. If I don't promise any of them, nobody
  can take one."
- Ghost :: The morning Marisol died. Alex promised nine. Three alarms, a
  long shower, a client message answered on the stairs. Eleven. She died at
  ten-forty.
- Lie :: Clocks are cruel, and looking at one is how you let it accuse you.
  Time cannot be held; planning it only makes losing it worse.
- Trouble :: Rent clears on the 1st. Two invoices are ninety days out and
  the studio that owes them has stopped answering.
- Psychological Weakness :: Will not attach an hour to any commitment.
  "Sometime this week." "I'll swing by." "Before end of day."
- Moral Weakness :: Spends Sam's mornings instead of their own. Asks Sam to
  take the 8:00 courier handover — the exact hour Sam has to be free for the
  care centre's call.

** Desire

- Goal :: Win the twelve-month Kestrel retainer. Pitch is at nine, three
  weeks out.
- Motivation :: That once the money is steady, the days will hold
  themselves. (Out of the Lie, so false underneath: it puts the shape of the
  day in the money's gift rather than Alex's.)
- Conflict :: Dana, who decides who gets the retainer and needs Alex
  available rather than booked.

** Need

- Goal :: Give the day a shape and keep one hour they promised.
- Motivation :: To be someone who can be counted on at a named hour — which
  is exactly what Sam has stopped counting on.
- Conflict :: The Lie, and what Alex would have to admit about the morning
  Marisol died: that the clock did not take that morning. Alex did.

** Plan

- Plan :: Copy Sam's routine exactly, to the minute, and be visibly reliable
  for three weeks so that Dana has no argument left.
- Fallback plan :: Take the 6:00 calls. Be so available that the retainer is
  cheaper to grant than to refuse.

** Aspects

- Aspect :: "I do my best work at two in the morning." True, and it is the
  sentence Dana uses to buy them.
- Aspect :: Cannot stand to watch someone else be late.
- Aspect :: Marisol's watch on their wrist, unwound.
- Relationship :: Sam from 4B has a key to Alex's flat, and Alex has never
  once had to ask twice.
- Possession :: The watch. Mechanical, and it stops unless it is wound by
  hand every day.

** Backstory

Alex is nineteen, at the kitchen table in Marisol's flat, and has been there
since seven because the bus times only work that way. Marisol comes in at
eight, makes tea for two without asking, and they sit. At ten to nine she
puts her cup down, says "I have the doctor at half past", stands, and goes.
Alex remembers the hour more clearly than anything she said, and has never
understood why the memory feels like an accusation.

** Interrogation

1. What would they never do?
   Say a time out loud and then not be there. It is the only promise Alex
   treats as unbreakable, which is why they have arranged a life in which
   they never make one.
2. What do they do when they are alone and nobody is watching?
   Count. Minutes to the door, minutes to the meeting, minutes left. Alex
   narrates their whole life in arithmetic and has never once written any of
   it down.
3. What are they wrong about?
   That the morning Marisol died was taken from them. It was spent.
4. What would they die for?
   Not being the reason someone else missed something.
5. Write them a trivial scene.
   Ordering coffee, Alex says "whatever's fastest", and then waits four
   minutes for the fastest thing while a filter coffee sits ready on the
   counter.

** Change Milestones

| Plot point         | Type       | What changes                                           |
|--------------------+------------+--------------------------------------------------------|
| 6. Crossing        | Adjustment | Writes "Day 1" and sets an alarm for a stated hour     |
| 9. Midpoint        | Adjustment | Stops asking permission to be unavailable; starts refusing hours |
| 10. Apparent Defeat | Adjustment | Learns what an hour of Sam's actually cost             |
| 11. Self-Revelation | Break      | Stance 1 → 5. The clock did not take that morning      |
| 13. Moral Decision | Break      | Keeps an hour at the price of the thing the hours were for |

** Notes

The counting is the voice. If a scene from Alex's point of view contains no
number, check whether it is really in their head.

* Dana Okonkwo
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: char-dana-okonkwo-001
:Role: Opponent
:Stance: Availability is loyalty
:Goal: Keep Alex on call rather than on retainer
:Age: 44
:Occupation: Founder and art director, Okonkwo & Field
:First-appearance: Chapter 2, Scene 2
:Weight: 2
:RelationshipsData: [[id:char-alex-rivera-001][Alex Rivera]](client,8,negative); [[id:char-priya-raman-001][Priya Raman]](employer,7,negative)
:END:

- Concept :: A woman who has not been late in twenty years and has not been
  free in twenty years, and can no longer tell the two facts apart.
- Stance :: "Structure is a luxury for people who already have money. The
  rest of us take the call."
- Ghost :: 2009. She kept the studio alive through the crash by being the
  one who answered, at any hour, for two years, and lost nothing except
  everything that was not the studio.
- Lie :: Availability is loyalty. Anyone protecting their hours is
  protecting them from her.
- Trouble :: Okonkwo & Field is one lost account from folding, and Kestrel
  is the account.
- Psychological Weakness :: Cannot let go of anything with a deadline on it,
  and so is the bottleneck in her own agency.
- Moral Weakness :: She buys other people's mornings with praise and calls
  it opportunity. Priya has been on a reachable-at-any-hour retainer for
  three years and has not taken a holiday in two.

** Desire

# The same Goal as the protagonist: both of them are after Alex's mornings.

- Goal :: Control of Alex's mornings — Alex on call for Kestrel, at any
  hour, rather than contracted to a fixed twelve-month scope.
- Motivation :: That a studio survives on the people who answer, and that
  she is protecting Alex from a retainer they are not steady enough to hold.
- Conflict :: Alex, once Alex starts naming hours.

** Need

- Goal :: To let one deadline out of her hands and watch it be met by
  someone else.
- Motivation :: The two years she does not talk about, and what answering
  every hour of them actually cost her.
- Conflict :: The Lie. If availability is not loyalty, then the two years
  were not loyalty either, and she has no name for what they were.

** Plan and counterattacks

Flatter the Weakness directly: Alex does their best work at two in the
morning, and Dana is the only client who understands that about them. When
Alex starts refusing hours, escalate — a 6:00 call booked as a courtesy, a
public compliment that makes refusal look like ingratitude, and finally the
pitch itself scheduled at nine on the one morning Alex has already promised
away.

** Aspects

- Aspect :: "I have never missed a deadline." Said as a credential, heard as
  a threat, and both are correct.
- Aspect :: Answers within four minutes, always, and everyone has noticed.
- Relationship :: Priya owes her a career and has been told so once, which
  was enough.
- Possession :: A phone that is never face-down.

** Backstory

March 2009, ten past one in the morning, Dana answers a call from a client
who is drunk and wants to talk about a logo. She talks about the logo for
fifty minutes. The account stays. She writes the time down in the margin of
the brief, and she still has the brief.

** Change Milestones

| Plot point          | Type       | What changes                                        |
|---------------------+------------+-----------------------------------------------------|
| 9. Midpoint         | Adjustment | Stops asking Alex and starts booking Alex           |
| 12. Battle          | Adjustment | Says the two years out loud for the first time      |
| 13. Moral Decision  | —          | Does not change Stance. She watches Alex leave and books the room for ten past |

** Notes

Her Stance has to be arguable. If a reader finishes the book thinking Dana
was simply wrong, the theme has been preached rather than proved: she is
right about the city, and wrong about what to do inside it.

* Sam Chen
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: char-sam-chen-001
:Role: Ally
:Stance: A routine is a discipline you owe yourself
:Goal: Get their father's day place confirmed
:First-appearance: Chapter 1, Scene 2
:Weight: 3
:RelationshipsData: [[id:char-alex-rivera-001][Alex Rivera]](neighbour,7,positive)
:END:

# Intermediate profile: reduced, plus Lie, Weakness and Relationship. Enough
# interior to carry the ally attack with authority.

- Concept :: The neighbour from 4B who looks like serenity and is in fact
  load-bearing.
- Stance :: "I keep the mornings because I am the sort of person who keeps
  them." (Opening. It becomes Stance 5 by the end, and the distance between
  the two is Sam's whole arc.)
- Lie :: That the routine is a virtue rather than a scaffold, and that
  needing it would be a weakness worth hiding.
- Trouble :: Their father had a stroke fourteen months ago. The care centre
  calls at 8:00 on Thursdays, and only at 8:00.
- Weakness :: Will not say that they need something. Will reorganize a whole
  week rather than ask.

** Own desire

# Their own, not auxiliary. It cannot be "help the protagonist".

- Goal :: Get the confirmed day place at the Bell Lane centre, which
  requires the Thursday calls and an assessment appointment in person.
- Motivation :: Four hours a day back, and a father who is somewhere with
  people in them.
- Conflict :: The centre's intake officer, a waiting list, and — as it turns
  out — Alex, who keeps spending the eight o'clock hour.

** Aspects

- Aspect :: "I've learned to spot the signs." Kind, observant, and a little
  proud of it.
- Aspect :: The notebook goes everywhere and is never shown to anyone.
- Relationship :: Alex has never asked what the notebook is for, and Sam has
  never volunteered.

** Change Milestones

| Plot point          | Type       | What changes                                     |
|---------------------+------------+--------------------------------------------------|
| 10. Apparent Defeat | Adjustment | Says out loud, once, what the hour was for       |
| 12. Battle          | Break      | Stance 4 → 5. Asks Alex for something directly   |

** Notes

Sam is not a mentor. Sam is a person running a tighter operation than Alex
for reasons Alex has not asked about, and the mentor reading is Alex's
mistake — one the reader should be able to catch before Alex does.

* Priya Raman
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: char-priya-raman-001
:Role: Fake-Ally Opponent
:Stance: Never owe anyone an hour
:Goal: Be the one Dana calls
:First-appearance: Chapter 3, Scene 2
:Weight: 4
:RelationshipsData: [[id:char-dana-okonkwo-001][Dana Okonkwo]](employer,7,negative); [[id:char-alex-rivera-001][Alex Rivera]](colleague,4,negative)
:END:

# Looks like an ally and serves the opponent. Reduced profile, both Desires.

- Concept :: The freelancer two floors down who complains about Dana more
  fluently than anyone and has never once said no to her.
- Stance :: "I keep my hours by making sure I never owe anybody one."
- Trouble :: Three years on a reachable-at-any-hour retainer, and the
  arithmetic only works if she stays the most reliable name on the list.

** Declared desire

- Goal :: Get Alex out from under Dana. Solidarity, drinks, war stories.
- Motivation :: Nobody should have to live like this, and she means it.
- Conflict :: Alex himself, without his knowing it — every hour Alex
  reclaims is an hour Priya has to cover.

** Real desire

- Goal :: The Kestrel retainer.
- Motivation :: Three years of being available is only worth something if it
  ends in a contract, and it has to end soon.
- Conflict :: Alex, who is better at the work and worse at everything else,
  and who has just started getting better at everything else.

** Reveal

- Who they really serve :: Dana, and before that herself. She has been
  passing Alex's free slots to Dana all along — which is how Dana always
  knew exactly when Alex could be booked.
- When they are revealed :: At the apparent defeat, in a forwarded message
  with the timestamps left on.

** Aspects

- Aspect :: "We should stick together." Sincere every time she says it, and
  it has never once cost her anything.

** Notes

The reveal has to be *documentary*, not narrated — a single focalizer means
the reader cannot be shown Priya deciding. They can be shown a timestamp.

* Casey Mbeki
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: char-casey-mbeki-001
:Role: Thematic Supporting
:Stance: Promise nobody an hour
:Goal: Get through the week without being noticed
:First-appearance: Chapter 8, Scene 2
:Weight: 5
:RelationshipsData: [[id:char-alex-rivera-001][Alex Rivera]](stranger,2,positive)
:END:

# One for every Stance you want represented. Casey carries Alex's opening
# Stance, which is what makes the last scene mean anything: the wheel is
# still turning, and Alex is now the other person in it.

- Concept :: Twenty-three, four months into a first job, and already
  convinced that the trick is to be reachable.
- Stance :: "Time is what other people take from you." (Alex's Stance 1,
  intact, in a new mouth.)
- Trouble :: A probation review in six weeks and a manager who messages at
  weekends.

** Desire

- Goal :: Get through probation without anyone deciding they are difficult.
- Motivation :: The job is the flat, and the flat is not negotiable.
- Conflict :: A manager whose only measure of commitment is response time.

** Aspects

- Aspect :: "It's fine, I wasn't doing anything." Said reflexively, and
  never true.

** Notes

Casey is not a lesson delivered. Alex offers, and does not preach — the
distinction is the whole scene, and Pass 2 checks for it.

* Marisol Rivera
:PROPERTIES:
:ID: char-marisol-rivera-001
:Role: Thematic Supporting
:Stance: The hours are not yours to keep, only to spend well
:Goal: (Dead before the story opens)
:First-appearance: Backstory only
:Weight: -1
:RelationshipsData: [[id:char-alex-rivera-001][Alex Rivera]](family,10,positive)
:END:

# :Weight: -1 — kept on file, left out of the character timeline.
#
# She appears only in the Ghost, in the watch, and in one remembered kitchen
# table. She should not occupy a column in a table of who is present in
# which scene, because the answer is "none" and a column of blanks reads as
# a linking failure. Everything else about her still works: search finds
# her, links resolve, and the timeline names her underneath itself.

- Concept :: Alex's grandmother, who was never once in a hurry and never
  once early.
- Stance :: "You don't get to keep them. You only get to choose what you
  spend them on."
- Trouble :: (None. She is the one person in this book who did not have any.)

** Desire

- Goal :: Morning tea, evening walk, Sunday phone call, in that order,
  every day, until she could not.
- Motivation :: That a day with a shape is a day you can hand to somebody.
- Conflict :: Time, which she lost on the terms everybody loses it on.

** Aspects

- Aspect :: "I have the doctor at half past." A sentence with an hour in it,
  said without apology, which Alex has never been able to say.

** Notes

She is the Stance the book argues for, held before the book starts, by
someone who never had to explain it. That is why she must not appear on the
page: the argument has to be made by Alex, not inherited.
