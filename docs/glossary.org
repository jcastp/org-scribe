* What this is

The authoritative Spanish↔English mapping for the writing method the
=novel-es= and =novel-en= template sets implement. Every template string,
message key and capture prompt in =novel-en= resolves through this table.

Two rules govern it:

1. *Where a term has a canonical English source, that source wins.* The method
   is a Spanish synthesis of predominantly English-language craft sources —
   Truby, Swain, McKee, Maass, Gardner, Browne & King, Save the Cat, and the
   FATE rulebook. Most of the vocabulary is therefore not translated but
   *restored*. Rows marked /fixed/ are not open to preference.
2. *Where no source exists, the choice was made once and is recorded with its
   reasoning* (see [[*The six judgement calls][The six judgement calls]]). Rows marked /settled/ may be revisited,
   but only deliberately — a synonym slipped in later re-creates the ambiguity
   the Spanish revision removed.

A third rule follows from both: *do not port terminology between the two sets
casually.* If a term needs to change, change it here first, then in both
template sets, then in the message alists.

* Property names are not translated

A distinction that trips people up. Three different things are localized
differently:

| Thing                    | Localized?                |
|--------------------------+---------------------------|
| Heading text and prose   | Yes — follows the project |
| *Scene* property names     | Yes — via the alias table |
| *Entity* property names    | *No* — English in both sets |
| Property *values* for =Role= | *No* — English in both sets |

So a Spanish project carries =* [Nombre del oponente]= with =:Role: Opponent=,
and its scenes carry =:Trama:= rather than =:Plot:=. Entity properties stay
English because three modules (=org-scribe-overlays.el=, column view, the health
report) read them as literals, and because they are machine-facing identifiers
a writer barely sees. Scene properties are localized because writers *do* see
them, in the property drawer and in column view, and because existing projects
already carry the Spanish spellings.

** Plotting methods (Copo vivo)

Vocabulary specific to the two plotting methods added beside the Sistema
(=org-scribe--methods=, =core/org-scribe-core.el=; design files under
=org-scribe-templates/methods/{helice,matriz}/=). Both are collectively the
*Copo vivo* ("Living Snowflake"): a rebuild of the Sistema's own Snowflake
scaffold that gives character its own leveled progression instead of letting
it fill in holes plot already made. La Hélice starts from a premise; la Matriz
starts from characters. Everything in the "Character chain" and "Plot"
sections above is shared unchanged — the causal chain, the thirteen
non-negotiables, the four quarters — since both methods are built on that same
vocabulary rather than replacing it.

*Tema/Theme and Postura/Stance are shared too, not Sistema-exclusive — only
their timing differs.* All three methods place "Theme Stated" among the
thirteen non-negotiables and use Stance in the character profile; what
differs is when a Theme is named and the Stance list built. The Sistema
front-loads both in its Núcleo, before any character exists ("at least five
Stances" is that Núcleo's own minimum, not a requirement of the concept
itself). La Hélice names the Theme at level 3, and la Matriz at level 4 —
in both cases exactly where that method's own Starting Gate sits, and
derived rather than declared: the Theme and its list of Stances are read off
the positions the living forces already hold (their Lies, argued Stances,
and — in la Hélice — the two-endings cross-check), not decided in advance
of the cast. A character's =:Stance:= is filled in earlier than that in
either method (the causal-chain profile, level 2 in both), so it is written
provisionally and checked against the list once the list exists — see the
capture and template comments on the =Stance= field.

Two calls specific to this vocabulary, following the same "settled" discipline
as the six below:

- *"Fuerza viva" is "living force", not "living character".* Both methods'
  own definition of the term is functional, not about being a rounded
  character — it names any character whose next wrong decisions can be
  deduced from their profile without consulting the plot — so the English
  keeps the same abstraction rather than narrowing it to "character".
- *Desastre and colisión are not merged into one English word*, even though
  they play the same structural role (a Lie put to the test, at a fixed
  point in the four-quarter structure) and the health report and the design
  templates treat them identically as prose. La Hélice designs a disaster and
  looks for its owner; la Matriz reads a collision off an already-filled
  matrix and the owner comes with it. Collapsing the distinction to a single
  term would erase the direction each method works in, which is the one
  fact about them everything else follows from.

Deliberately *not* glossed here, because Q1 (=diffsystems.org=) keeps them as
prose rather than a linked entity type: no scene property, no capture
template, no health check reads =Desastre=/=Disaster= or =Colisión=/=Collision=,
so unlike every other row in this file they are not resolved through any
module's alias table — only through the design file's own prose.

* The six judgement calls

The only terms not dictated by a source. Kept with their reasoning because a
glossary without it gets relitigated by whoever touches it next.

1. *Postura → Stance.* Short, distinct from "Theme", takes a possessive
   naturally ("Orlando's stance"). /Position/ was rejected: it is closer to the
   rejected Spanish /Posición Temática/ and risks re-importing the exact
   confusion the merge removed.
2. *Escollo → Hazard.* Nothing canonical exists. Pairs acoustically with /Echo/
   and reads as a property of a place. /Snag/ is too small for something that
   worsens the World Problem; /Reef/ is the literal translation and too
   nautical outside one worked example.
3. *Puerta de salida → The Starting Gate.* The Spanish means the door you leave
   planning through; a literal /Exit Gate/ reads in English as an ending. The
   racing sense carries both "this is the threshold" and "now you run". The one
   term where the English is arguably better than the Spanish.
4. *Debilidad → Weakness, not Flaw.* Truby says weakness, and /Debilidad/ is
   literally that. Cost: the pre-existing message keys said =flaw=; renamed
   while the capture builders were being rewritten anyway.
5. *Protagonista → Protagonist.* Truby says /hero/, but /hero/ carries a moral
   charge this method explicitly refuses — one worked example ends in Ascenso
   and loses, the other in Caída and wins. /Protagonist/ stays neutral.
6. *Irrenunciables → Non-Negotiables.* The method's own gloss is an argument
   ("sin ellos la historia no es completa ni causal"), and /non-negotiable/
   carries an argument. /Essentials/ is blander; /Indispensables/ is a false
   friend.

* Terms deliberately left alone

- =:capa:= and =:ref:= tags exist only in the method document, which does not
  ship with org-scribe. If an English method document is ever exported,
  =:layer:= / =:ref:= is the obvious pair.
- =Línea principal= / =Main Line= and =Subtrama= / =Subplot= are heading text in
  the plot file, and read as names rather than classifications; the
  classification lives in =:THREAD-TYPE:=.
